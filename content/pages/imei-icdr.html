Two services

Which one are you?

Almost everyone arrives saying "I need IMEI certification". It means two different things depending on whether you're registering IMEIs that already exist or creating new ones. The first is mandatory and time-critical. Find yourself below.

Mandatory · importers & manufacturers

IMEI ICDR compliance — mandatory for importers and manufacturers

For you if: you import devices carrying IMEIs — your own brand or a third party's — or you manufacture devices and sell them in India. Both are covered. There is no version of this that only applies to importers.

  • Importers: registration is what allows the device in. Do it before the consignment moves, and note there is no exemption for third-party brands
  • Manufacturers: if you build devices and sell them in India, you register too. Domestic production is not exempt
  • Applies to every device with a SIM or eSIM, not only handsets
  • You do not need your own TAC for this if the devices already carry valid IMEIs

We handle the filing so it's right first time. Most of the pain in ICDR is a rejected or incomplete filing, not the rule itself.

Creating your own IMEI numbers

IMEI certification — brand registration & TAC allocation

For you if: you manufacture the device, or someone else makes it under your brand, and you want your own IMEI numbers.

  • Your brand is registered with the GSMA, the global registry
  • The brand name goes through approval — the step that decides your timeline
  • Once approved, you apply for the TAC — the 8-digit code for your model
  • IMEI numbers are generated from that TAC for your production run

We consult through the whole sequence, from the first GSMA registration to the IMEIs in your hands.

Some businesses need both — but fewer than you'd think

If you're launching your own brand and importing it into India, you need both: brand registration and a TAC to create the IMEIs, then ICDR registration to bring the devices in. If you're distributing devices someone else made, ICDR registration on its own is usually the whole requirement. Who needs this

Anything with a SIM or eSIM, not just phones

The requirement follows cellular connectivity, not the product category. Plenty of companies find that out late.

Mobile handsets & tablets

Feature phones, smartphones, cellular tablets. The original case, and the most scrutinised at customs.

GPS & asset trackers

Vehicle trackers, fleet units, AIS 140 devices, container and cold-chain monitors. All cellular, all in scope.

Routers, dongles & CPE

4G/5G routers, MiFi units, USB dongles, fixed wireless CPE — anything that takes a data SIM.

IoT & M2M modules

Smart meters, POS terminals, vending and telematics units, industrial sensors. Module makers and device makers both need to get this right.

Wearables with eSIM

Smartwatches, kids' trackers, medical wearables. eSIM counts exactly the same as a physical SIM slot.

Importers & brand owners

Bringing in devices made by someone else, or selling under your own label. You still carry the registration obligation in India.

How a TAC actually gets issued

Eight steps, and most applications stall at step four

This is the real sequence, not a simplified version. Knowing where the delays sit is most of the value.

1

Primary registration

Your company is registered with the GSMA as an applicant. Entity documents, authorised contacts, the account itself.

2

Brand name application

The brand your devices will carry has to be applied for separately. This is not automatic and not instant.

3

Documents uploaded

Trademark evidence, incorporation papers, authorisations. Weak trademark evidence is the single biggest cause of rejection.

4

Brand name approved

The step applications stall on. A name too close to an existing brand, or thin ownership proof, and you go back to step two.

5

TAC profile prepared

The device profile — model, radio bands, form factor, technical parameters. It has to match what you'll actually ship.

6

TAC allocated

Your 8-digit code is issued against that model.

7

IMEIs generated

IMEI numbers are produced from the TAC for your production run, with the check digit computed correctly.

8

ICDR registration

The IMEI ranges are registered with the DoT so the devices work on Indian networks and clear customs.

Why it matters

An unregistered device is a brick on an Indian network

This isn't paperwork that catches up with you at an audit. If the IMEI isn't registered, the device can simply stop working for your customer — and the complaint lands with you, not with the network.

Customs also check at import. A consignment of devices without registered IMEIs is a consignment that doesn't move.

What enforcement looks like

The DoT has been explicit that IMEI registration is mandatory and that tampering with or duplicating IMEIs is an offence. Devices with unregistered, duplicated or invalid IMEIs can be blocked from Indian networks through CEIR.

Enforcement runs through the DoT, customs at the point of import, and local agencies.

FAQ

Questions we get on the first call

What is a TAC, and how is it different from an IMEI?

The TAC — Type Allocation Code — is the first 8 digits of every IMEI, and it identifies the device model. The GSMA issues one TAC per model to the brand owner.

The full IMEI is 15 digits: your 8-digit TAC, then a 6-digit serial number unique to each unit, then a check digit. So you get one TAC and generate many IMEIs from it.

What is ICDR registration?

ICDR stands for Indian Counterfeited Device Restriction. It's the Department of Telecommunications' system for recording the IMEIs of devices made in or imported into India, before they're sold. Registration happens on the DoT's portal.

It exists to keep counterfeit and duplicate-IMEI devices off Indian networks. If your device isn't on it, it can be blocked.

We import devices made by a third party. Do we need our own TAC?

If the devices already carry valid IMEIs from the manufacturer's TAC, you don't need your own TAC — but you do need the IMEI ranges registered under ICDR before the consignment lands.

If you're selling under your own brand, that's different: the brand owner generally needs their own TAC. Tell us which situation you're in and we'll map it.

How long does TAC allocation take?

The mechanical steps are quick. What sets the timeline is brand name approval, which depends on your trademark position and how close your name is to existing ones. A clean application with a registered trademark moves quickly; a contested name can take weeks of back-and-forth.

We'll give you a realistic view on the first call once we've seen your brand and trademark status — not a number designed to win the enquiry.

Is a separate TAC required for every model and variant?

Generally yes. One TAC covers one device model. Variants that differ in radio configuration or hardware usually need their own. Cosmetic variants sometimes don't.

Send us your model list and we'll tell you how many TACs you actually need, which is often fewer than people expect.

Does this apply to IoT and M2M devices, or only to phones?

Yes. The requirement follows cellular connectivity. A smart meter, a GPS tracker, a POS terminal or an industrial sensor with a SIM or eSIM needs IMEIs exactly as a handset does.

This catches a lot of IoT companies late, usually when the first consignment reaches customs.

We don't have a registered trademark yet. Does that block us?

Not necessarily, but it changes the sequence. A pending trademark application is usually workable. No filing at all is the most common reason brand approval is refused.

Raise it on the first call and we'll advise on the order to do things in. Handled early this costs you little; discovered at the approval stage it can cost two months.

Our application was rejected by a previous consultant. Can you take it over?

Yes, and it's a common request. Share the correspondence from the GSMA or the portal — the query raised, the rejection notice, and the application reference.

We'll assess whether the file can be corrected and resubmitted or whether a fresh application is the faster route, and tell you which before you commit to either.
