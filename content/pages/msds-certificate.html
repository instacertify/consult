Read this before you pay anyone

Nobody issues an "MSDS certificate". Here's what you're actually buying.

No government department, lab or authority in India issues an MSDS certificate. A Safety Data Sheet is a document that you, as the manufacturer or supplier, are responsible for — and it has to be accurate, in the right format, and kept current. What a consultant sells you is the work of writing it properly.

We still call it an MSDS certificate on this page, because that's what everyone asks for. But you should know what you're getting, and you should be suspicious of anyone selling you a "3-year validity" on a document that has no expiry date.

What you'll be told elsewhere
  • "Valid 3 years, renew every 3 years" — no expiry exists; it changes only when your product or your market's rules change
  • "Issued by a lab" — labs issue test reports, not safety data sheets
  • A template with your product name dropped in, and hazard classification left generic
  • One version for every market, when the EU, US and China each want it differently
What you get from us
  • Real hazard classification from your actual composition, not a guess from the product category.
  • Written for the destination. Tell us where it ships and we write it to that regime.
  • Label elements too — pictograms, signal word, hazard and precautionary statements.
  • We tell you when it genuinely needs updating — when your formula changes, or when the rules move in a market you sell into.

The rulebook we work from

Drafted to UN GHS. Adapted to the market you're selling into.

Every sheet we write starts from the UN GHS purple book — the rulebook the whole system is built on. What is the validity of an MSDS certificate? Do I renew it every 3 years?

No. This is the most common piece of misinformation in this market. A Safety Data Sheet has no expiry date and no renewal fee. It isn't a licence issued by anyone, so there is nothing to renew.

It stays valid until something changes. The two things that change it are: your product — a new formulation, a new supplier, a new pack — or the rules in the market you sell into, when the local authority there adopts a new GHS revision or updates its classification requirements.

That's why we ask which countries you ship to. If the rules move in one of your markets, the sheet for that market needs updating. Not on a three-year timer, and not for every market at once.

What is the full form of MSDS, and how is it different from SDS?

MSDS is Material Safety Data Sheet. SDS is Safety Data Sheet, the name used under GHS, the globally harmonised system.

They're the same document in practice. The older MSDS had no fixed structure and varied by supplier and country; the modern SDS has the mandatory 16-section format. India and much of Asia still say MSDS out of habit. What we write is a GHS-format SDS — which is what your buyer wants, whatever they call it.

Do I need an MSDS for export?

If your product is a chemical or contains one, almost certainly yes. Your buyer, freight forwarder, airline or the customs authority at the destination will ask for it, and the shipment waits until you produce one.

Even for products that aren't classified as hazardous, buyers frequently ask for a sheet confirming exactly that. We write those too.

Will you sign an NDA before I share my formulation?

Yes, and we'd suggest it. We can't classify a mixture properly without the real composition, so we ask for it, and we understand that your formulation is the business. Send us an NDA or ask for ours before you share anything.

Can I write the SDS myself?

You can. It's your document and there's no rule saying a consultant has to write it. What people find is that hazard classification of a mixture, the transport section and getting the destination country's format right take a lot longer than expected, and a rejected sheet costs more in shipping delays than the sheet cost.

If you have one already and just want it checked, say so. We'll review it and tell you whether it needs a rewrite or a correction.

My supplier gave me an SDS. Can I just use that?

Sometimes, but check two things. First, is it for the exact product you're shipping, or for one raw material inside it? Second, is your company named in Section 1 as the supplier? If you're putting your name on the product, the sheet needs to be yours.

Do you handle UN 38.3 for batteries?

Yes. Lithium cells and packs need an SDS alongside the UN 38.3 test summary before a courier or airline will accept them. When you're going through certification for a battery product, that summary forms part of the pack.

We prepare the SDS and arrange the testing through ISO/IEC 17025-accredited partner laboratories, so it comes from one place.
