Before you buy a certificate

G-Mark does not get your goods through Saudi customs

This is the call we take most often, usually from someone whose container is already at Jeddah or Dammam. They hold a valid G-Mark certificate. They assumed it was the key to the Gulf. It is not.

G-Mark is the Gulf-wide technical layer — it says your product meets the GSO technical regulation for its category. Each member state then runs its own national conformity system on top, and that is the one customs actually checks. Saudi Arabia wants a PCoC for the product and an SCoC for the shipment through the SABER platform. The UAE wants ECAS registration for regulated categories. Neither is replaced by your G-Mark certificate, and neither can be arranged quickly once the goods have sailed.

The basics, stated plainly

What the Gulf Conformity Mark actually is

Who runs it

The GCC Standardization Organization, GSO. It publishes the Gulf technical regulations and approves the Notified Bodies that assess products against them.

Where it's valid

Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, plus Yemen. One assessment, one certificate, seven markets — which is the real saving in it.

What goes on the product

The tracking symbol — the G-Mark itself, the four-digit Notified Body number and a QR code so customs can verify the certificate. Minimum 5 mm tall, permanently affixed.

What it covers today

Low voltage electrical equipment and appliances under BD-142004-01, children's toys under BD-131704-01, and water-consuming products under the newer conservation regulation.

Two routes, not one

Some products go by manufacturer declaration with factory control and line testing. Others need a Notified Body and a Gulf Type Examination certificate. The product list decides.

How long it lasts

Certificates are typically issued for a defined term and renewed. The declaration of conformity itself has to be kept for at least ten years from the date of manufacture.

Four things exporters get wrong

Most G-Mark money is wasted before any testing happens

1

Buying a mark the product never needed

Cosmetics, automotive parts, medical devices and machinery above the low voltage range are all routinely certified to G-Mark by exporters who did not need it. The scope is set by the technical regulation and the HS code, not by whether a certification body is willing to sell you one.

2

Treating the Gulf as one market

The G-Mark layer is genuinely Gulf-wide and that is its value. The national layer is not. Saudi Arabia's SABER, with a product certificate and a separate shipment certificate, is a different exercise from the UAE's ECAS, and neither is covered by the other.

3

Leaving Arabic to the end

The declaration of conformity, the safety manual and the labelling are required in Arabic. This is a compliance requirement with technical content, not a translation job — and a manual run through machine translation the week before shipping is how files come back for correction.

4

Retesting what's already tested

For low voltage products, an IECEE CB certificate and report carries a presumption of conformity for much of the safety assessment. If you already hold one for CE or another market, a large part of the work is done — and paying to repeat it is a pure loss.

How the Gulf actually works

Two layers, and you need both

Get this picture right and the rest of the project plans itself.

Layer one — G-MarkDone once, serves all seven markets

The Gulf technical regulation for your product category. Testing, the technical file, the risk analysis, the Arabic declaration, and a Notified Body assessment where the route requires one. This is the part that genuinely is Gulf-wide — you do it once, and it does not need repeating per country.

Layer two — Saudi ArabiaSABER, under the SALEEM programme

A Product Certificate of Conformity registered against your Saudi customs tariff code and valid for one year, then a Shipment Certificate of Conformity for each consignment, which can only be issued if the PCoC is live. No SCoC, no clearance. This is the single most common reason Indian exporters get stuck at a Saudi port.

Layer two — United Arab EmiratesECAS, with EQM optional

The Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme is mandatory for regulated product categories and is administered through the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology. The Emirates Quality Mark is voluntary and assesses your factory as well as your product — worth having when a retailer or a government buyer asks for it, and not otherwise.

Layer two — Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, OmanNational import conformity

Lighter than Saudi Arabia, but not nothing. Each runs its own import conformity documentation, Arabic labelling requirements and attestation rules, and public-sector buyers frequently add conditions of their own in the purchase order. We confirm the current position per destination rather than working from last year's experience.

Where it goes wrongSequencing, almost always

Companies that treat every country as a fresh project pay for the same testing three or four times and still miss a deadline somewhere. One set of test reports and one Arabic documentation pack can feed the whole Gulf — but only if it is built that way from the first day.

FAQ

Questions we get on the first call

What is G-Mark certification?

The Gulf Conformity Mark is the conformity marking of the GCC Standardization Organization. It shows that a product meets the applicable Gulf technical regulation for its category.

It covers low voltage electrical equipment and appliances under BD-142004-01, children's toys under BD-131704-01, and more recently water-consuming products. The mark is recognised across Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman and Yemen, which is where its value lies — one assessment rather than seven.

Is G-Mark enough to sell in Saudi Arabia?

No, and this is the most expensive misunderstanding in Gulf trade.

Saudi Arabia operates the SABER platform under its SALEEM programme. Regulated products need a Product Certificate of Conformity, registered against the Saudi customs tariff code and valid for one year, and then a Shipment Certificate of Conformity for each individual consignment. The SCoC can only be issued where a live PCoC already exists, and without the SCoC the goods do not clear.

Your G-Mark certificate supports the conformity assessment. It does not substitute for either of those.

Do I need G-Mark for the UAE if I already have ECAS?

They serve different purposes and neither replaces the other.

ECAS is the UAE's own mandatory scheme for regulated product categories and is what gets your product legally onto the UAE market. G-Mark is the Gulf-wide layer, which matters if you are selling across the region rather than into the UAE alone.

If the UAE is your only Gulf market, ECAS may be all you need. If you are also shipping to Saudi Arabia, Kuwait or the others, doing the G-Mark work once is usually the cheaper path.

Which products actually need G-Mark?

Scope is set by the technical regulation and determined in practice by the HS code, not by the product description.

The low voltage regulation covers equipment with a nominal supply between 50 and 1000 V AC or 75 and 1500 V DC — household appliances, kitchen equipment, air conditioning, lighting, power tools, chargers and adaptors. Specifically excluded are medical and radiology equipment, lift components, electricity meters, equipment for ships, aircraft and railways, and equipment for explosive atmospheres.

Cosmetics, food, vehicles and tyres sit outside the scheme entirely and take different routes.

Do I need a Notified Body?

It depends on which list your product falls into, and that is not a choice you get to make.

Some products go by manufacturer declaration, supported by factory production control and production line testing. Others require a Notified Body to carry out a technical design examination and issue a Gulf Type Examination certificate before the mark can be applied.

Toys are assessed particularly carefully because of the mandatory risk analysis. We establish which route applies to your product before quoting, because it is the single biggest factor in the cost.

How long does G-Mark certification take?

For a straightforward low voltage product with existing test reports, plan for a few weeks. Where full testing is needed, add the lab queue and the sample logistics.

Two things stretch the timeline more than people expect: preparing the Arabic documentation properly, and the national registration afterwards. If you have a shipment date committed, tell us on the first call and we will work backwards from it and say plainly whether it is achievable.

Does my CB certificate help?

For low voltage products, considerably.

An IECEE CB certificate and report carries a presumption of conformity for much of the safety assessment under the Gulf low voltage regulation. If you already hold one — commonly you will, if you have done CE marking or sold into other CB-scheme markets — a large part of the testing work is already complete.

Send it with your first enquiry. It is the quickest way to reduce the quote.

What does the mark look like on the product?

What goes on the product is the tracking symbol, not just the letter mark.

It carries the G-Mark itself, the four-digit identification number of the Notified Body where one was involved, and a QR code that lets customs officers and buyers verify the certificate is live. It must be at least 5 mm high, permanently affixed, and clearly visible on the product, its label or its packaging.

We supply the artwork at the correct proportions so it does not get redrawn incorrectly on your packaging.
